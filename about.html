About

The 40-page PDF, translated

Every Indian exam notification hides the same two paragraphs somewhere around page 12: a photo spec and a signature spec, written like they don't expect you to actually find them. FormReady exists because someone should have found them for you already.

The problem

The document isn't the hard part. Finding the rules is.

None of this is complicated. It's just scattered, inconsistent, and buried on purpose in a document nobody reads for fun.

No two portals agree

IBPS wants your photo under 50 KB. SSC wants the same photo under 50 KB but in a different pixel box. One exam rejects PNG outright; another doesn't care. None of this is written down in one place — it's scattered across a PDF you have to hunt through while a deadline counts down.

The notification isn't written for you

It's a legal document written for auditors, not applicants. The line that actually matters to you — "signature must not be in capital letters" — sits between paragraphs about reservation policy and exam-day conduct rules. You have to read all 40 pages to trust you didn't miss the one that disqualifies you.

You find out too late

Most people discover the exact spec only after the portal rejects their upload — at 11pm, an hour before the window closes, with no idea whether the fix is "compress it" or "start over with a different photo." FormReady exists so that moment happens at the start of your evening, not the end of it.

What we actually do

Two problems, solved in order

1. Get your files to the exact spec

Application Mode tells you the real number — not "compress your photo," but "20–50 KB, 200×230 px, JPG, white background" for this specific exam. Then the Tools page does the actual compressing, resizing, and converting, in your browser, in seconds.

2. Get the form itself right

Specs solve the upload. They don't solve the thirty other fields — name exactly as your Class 10 certificate, category locked forever after you click submit, post preferences you can't reorder later. That's what the Fill Guide is for: a field-by-field companion so you're not improvising against a legal document at 11pm.

How we work

The rules we don't break

Privacy by default

Every tool runs client-side — canvas, JavaScript, your browser's own memory. Your photo, signature and PDF files are never uploaded, never touched by a server, never seen by us. Not "we delete it after," not "we encrypt it in transit" — it simply never leaves your device.

Numbers, not vibes

Every spec on the Applications page is compiled from the exam's own official notification, with the date we checked it and a link to the source PDF sitting right next to it. If we can't find a number in the official document, we say so — we don't quietly round it off from a blog post and call it fact.

Honest about gaps

Some notifications genuinely don't publish an exact px/KB figure — UPSC's OTR photo rules, for instance, live on a separate portal page, not the PDF itself. When that happens, Application Mode says so explicitly instead of inventing a plausible-sounding number. A wrong guess costs you your application; an honest "we don't know, check here" doesn't.

Origin

Built in Bangalore, out of one bad night

FormReady started in Bangalore, India, after a 20 KB photo limit turned a ten-minute task into a scramble at 11pm the night before a deadline — not because the photo was hard to take, but because nothing on the portal said what "20 KB" actually meant in pixels, or why the same photo that worked for one exam got silently rejected by another. The fix that night was a scrappy compressor built to hit an exact number. FormReady is that same idea, grown into an actual answer for every exam we can get a real notification for — still solo, still built by someone who's filled out these forms under the same pressure you have.

Built for the aspirants preparing for these exams — and for the coaching partners guiding them — to be a tool worth trusting with something as unforgiving as a submission deadline.
